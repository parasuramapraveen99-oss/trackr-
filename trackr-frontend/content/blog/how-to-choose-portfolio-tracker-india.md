# How to choose a portfolio tracker: a practical checklist

Use a small, representative set of investment records to evaluate a tracker. Include a partial sale, a fully exited investment and a documented corporate action if your history has them.

## Six questions to answer

| Question | Evidence to ask for |
|---|---|
| Does it understand my files? | An import of your actual broker format, not just a logo on a list |
| Can I repeat an import? | A second upload without duplicate trades |
| Can I trace a return? | Dates, cash flows, valuation basis and exclusions |
| What happens when history is missing? | A named gap, without a made-up purchase or zero price |
| What does reconciliation prove? | Account, statement date, coverage and the source checked |
| Can I leave? | Working export/deletion controls and a clear privacy policy |

## Look beyond the demonstration

A family-portfolio mockup is not evidence that permissions and consolidation are released. A model-portfolio chart is not evidence of a real backtest. A statement checker is not necessarily a transaction import.

## Compare cost after the workflow

Ask providers for current pricing, included portfolios and support terms. Subscription prices alone do not describe the work required to maintain your records.

# Trackr+, MProfit and iRecord: compare the workflow, not just the feature list

A portfolio tracker is useful when it answers your questions from records you can check. A feature checklist alone will not tell you how much work it takes to get there.

## Start with your records

Trackr+ focuses on replaying investment transactions into holdings, gains and returns. You bring broker files or forward supported contract notes; the application shows missing history and unpriced holdings rather than treating them as zero.

MProfit describes consolidated reporting across asset classes, statement processing and capital-gains reporting on its [official website](https://mprofit.in/). iRecord describes portfolio management and accounting in its [official product manual](https://irecordinfo.com/iRecord%20Manual.pdf). Those broader accounting workflows deserve a closer look if they are part of your daily work.

## Compare the same tasks

| Task | What to check in a demonstration |
|---|---|
| Import your records | Does your actual broker file work, including a repeat upload? |
| Understand returns | Are the dates, cash flows and exclusions visible? |
| Review dividends | Are announced amounts kept separate from received income? |
| Prepare reports | Can you trace a reported gain to its acquisition and sale? |
| Consolidate a family | Are ownership and client permissions actually available? |

Try those tasks with a small set of your own records. Do not assume that an unlisted feature is absent from another product.

## Where Trackr+ currently draws the line

The mutual-fund statement checker is a preview: it reads and checks a detailed statement, but does not save those transactions into your portfolio. Family and model-portfolio demonstrations are not evidence of a released client-management service.

Ask each provider for current pricing and plan limits directly. This comparison makes no claim that one product is universally cheaper or a replacement for another's accounting functions.

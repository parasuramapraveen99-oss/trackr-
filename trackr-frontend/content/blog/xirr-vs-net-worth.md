# XIRR and portfolio value answer different questions

Portfolio value tells you what the included investments are worth at a date. XIRR describes the annualised rate consistent with dated cash flows and the ending value. Adding more money can increase the first without improving the second.

## A simple distinction

Suppose a portfolio grows from ₹1 lakh to ₹1.5 lakh. That is a 50% increase in value. If you added ₹50,000 during the period, the value change alone does not establish a 50% investment return.

To calculate a money-weighted return, the dates matter too. The same purchase made near the beginning and near the end of a year commits capital for different lengths of time.

## Know the portfolio boundary

Trackr+'s securities performance uses recorded security cash flows and holdings. It is not your complete net worth: property, liabilities, idle broker cash and assets outside the included scope need their own records.

Recorded dividend income and charges can affect the result. Expected dividends do not count as received cash.

## Read the unit and the period

An annualised XIRR and the return over a short period are different presentations. Trackr+ labels short-window money-weighted returns as returns over the period. Missing data or an undefined return should remain unavailable rather than turning into zero.

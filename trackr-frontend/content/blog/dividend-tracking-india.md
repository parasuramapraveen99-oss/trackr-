# Dividend tracking: announced, expected and received are different numbers

A dividend announcement tells you what a company declared. It does not establish that cash arrived in your account.

That distinction matters when you read a portfolio return. Counting an expected payment as received income would add cash that the records do not yet support.

## Four stages to keep separate

| Stage | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| Declared | The company announced a dividend per share. |
| Expected | Your evidenced eligible holding implies an amount you should receive. |
| Received | A payment record establishes that cash arrived. |
| Recorded in the ledger | A dividend transaction can enter the return calculation. |

Trackr+ keeps these stages separate. Declared and expected amounts do not automatically become realised income. Receipt evidence and any tax withheld need their own records.

## Why the two totals can differ

The declared rate, your eligible quantity and the cash credited can refer to different facts. A statement may show a net amount after withholding. An announcement can also be corrected. Keep the amount, relevant dates and supporting document together before recording income.

## Read returns with their coverage

Only dividend transactions recorded in the ledger enter Trackr+'s money-weighted return and contribution calculations. If no receipt was recorded, an expected-dividend panel does not make the return a complete total-return figure.

A two-source check of an announcement can help detect a transcription error. It still does not prove a personal payment.
